## Today

### Today

`?view=today`

**What it is.** Your daily log. Add tasks/events/notes with the bullet grammar (t/e/n · * important · ^ memory · #tag), rate mood/stress/sleep, and write a gratitude line and a daily memory. The top cards summarise what you covered yesterday and any make-up work left over.

**Why it exists.** A day you did not write down is a day you cannot learn from — this page is what the habit is actually made of.

**How to use it.**

1. Type one line and press Enter. Start it with t, e or n to pick task, event or note; * makes it important, #tag files it.
2. Click a bullet glyph to cycle its status — open, done, migrated, dropped.
3. Before bed, set mood, stress and sleep. Every chart in Insights is built from those three numbers.
4. Write one gratitude line and one memory. That is the whole daily ritual, and it takes about a minute.
5. The Habits tab is the month grid: add a habit, then tap a cell to mark the day. Count habits increment toward a target instead of just toggling.
6. Read the consistency percentage rather than the current streak — a streak forgives nothing and teaches less.

## Plan

### Week

*Page title: Plan.* 
`?view=plan`

**What it is.** The migration workhorse: clear overdue open tasks (sort by date/priority, star the important ones, move or drop them). Define recurring daily/weekly tasks, and import .ics calendar events onto your Monthly.

**Why it exists.** Open tasks that quietly roll forward forever are how a journal dies; migration forces exactly one decision per task.

**How to use it.**

1. Open Plan at the end of a week or a month — that is when it earns its place.
2. Work the overdue list: move each task to today or tomorrow, or drop it. Dropping is a valid answer and half the point of the ritual.
3. Add recurring tasks so the things you do every day stop needing to be typed.
4. Import a .ics file to bring an existing calendar onto your Monthly.

### Month

*Page title: Monthly.* 
`?view=monthly`

**What it is.** A calendar of the month. Events appear as dots; a ribbon shows each day’s habit completion and mood tints the cell. Tap any day to open it. Below: this month’s location, goals, and photo.

**Why it exists.** A month at a glance is the only view that shows shape — which weeks you lost, and whether the gaps line up with anything.

**How to use it.**

1. Tap any day to open it in Today.
2. Record where you are this month and what you want out of it.
3. Add the photo of the month with a caption — it is the thing you will actually come back for.

### Goals

`?view=goals`

**What it is.** A cross-view rollup of every active target · habit weekly goals, fitness minutes, challenges, training-program days, and your streak · as progress bars. Tap a row to jump to its home view.

**Why it exists.** Targets scattered across seven pages are targets nobody reads; this is the single list, and it is read-only on purpose.

**How to use it.**

1. Read the bars top to bottom. Each one is an active target you set somewhere else.
2. Tap a row to jump to the page that owns it, and change it there.
3. A target you expected and cannot find has not been set yet — set it on its home page.

## Body

### Fitness

`?view=fitness`

**What it is.** Record today’s session and see whether the week is on track. The mode toggle filters everything: cardio asks for duration and distance, strength asks for sets, and the facts, stats and copy all follow. Pull-ups, home workouts and pickleball are activities on the select — Pull-ups and Pickleball also have their own Body tab for the program and the match record. The training calendar shows the last 26 weeks.

**Why it exists.** The week, not the session, is the unit that decides whether training is working — and you cannot judge a week from memory.

**How to use it.**

1. Pick the mode first. Cardio asks for duration and distance; strength asks for sets. The facts and the copy follow it.
2. Log the session while you are still warm. A session reconstructed at night is a session of guesses.
3. Check the week against its minutes target before deciding whether today is a rest day.

### Strength

*Page title: Strength tools.* 
`?view=gym`

**What it is.** The deep strength toolkit: searchable exercise picker, per-set weight/reps/RPE/type, 1RM hints, training-volume charts, plate calculator, muscle map with form/injury cues, personal records, saved routines, and progress photos. The 12-week hypertrophy block has its own tab — see Program.

**Why it exists.** Sets, reps and load only mean something next to what you lifted last time — holding that comparison is the whole job of this page.

**How to use it.**

1. Search the exercise picker and log your sets: weight, reps, RPE, set type.
2. Use the previous-session and live-1RM hints to choose today’s load instead of guessing it.
3. Open the muscle map for form cues and injury watch-outs before a lift that is new to you.
4. Take a progress photo now and again — the first-vs-latest compare is the only honest one.

### Program

*Page title: 12-Week Hypertrophy Block.* 
`?view=program`

**What it is.** A twelve-week push/pull/legs hypertrophy programme, run in three four-week phases. Pick the phase and the day, check exercises off as you go (partial days are fine), and type what you actually managed against each target — the tracker keeps both. "Load into session" hands the day's lifts to Strength's logger so you do not retype them. Progress is shared with the day count on Goals.

**Why it exists.** Twelve weeks is longer than motivation lasts; something you can put down and resume is what carries you across the gap.

**How to use it.**

1. Pick the phase and the day — six days a week, three four-week phases.
2. Check exercises off as you go. A partial day is recorded as a partial day, not a failure.
3. Type what you actually managed against each target. The tracker keeps both numbers, which is what makes the block reviewable later.
4. Use “Load into session” to hand the day’s lifts to Strength so you do not retype them.

### Pickleball

`?view=pickleball`

**What it is.** Log pickleball sessions (singles/doubles, games won/lost, duration, RPE). See your record · sessions, games, win %, day streak · plus a win-rate trend, win/loss split, games-per-week, and physio/trainer injury-prevention notes.

**Why it exists.** A pickleball session is a match record — format, partner, games won and lost — and none of that survives being logged as a duration.

**How to use it.**

1. Log the session: singles or doubles, games won and lost, duration, RPE.
2. Read win % and the win-rate trend rather than the session count. Playing more is not the same as playing better.
3. Read the physio notes before you decide to play through something.

### Pull-ups

`?view=pullups`

**What it is.** A Body tab. Your max sets your training set, ladder and pyramid; the recorder turns "three ladders to four" into the sets it stores, as a normal workout the rest of the app can read. Below: history, a twenty-six-week rep calendar, and the training manual — form, principles, the ability ladder, fourteen workout formats, nine progressions with demos, and equipment.

**Why it exists.** A pull-up session is a workout, but this page is the program, the calculator and the manual — none of which fit behind a duration field.

**How to use it.**

1. Enter your current max. It sets your training set, your ladder and your pyramid for you.
2. Record a session in the scheme’s own words — “three ladders to four” — and it stores the real sets underneath.
3. Work the manual below when you stall: form, the ability ladder, fourteen workout formats, nine progressions with demos.

### Coaching

`?view=coaching`

**What it is.** A full pickleball coaching curriculum, not just tracking: a 12-week beginner→4.0 program with a day-by-day practice schedule, a skill ladder (2.0→4.5+), a drill library by skill, and a mental-game track to build the right mindset. Start the program to track your week.

**Why it exists.** Tracking tells you what happened. A curriculum tells you what to do on Tuesday, which is the harder and more useful question.

**How to use it.**

1. Find your level on the skill ladder honestly (2.0 → 4.5+) before picking a week.
2. Start the 12-week beginner→4.0 program to track your week against a day-by-day schedule.
3. Pull drills from the library by the skill you are missing, not the one you enjoy.

### Nutrition

`?view=nutrition`

**What it is.** Log what you ate — pick from a food library (American + Indian staples) or set calories and macros directly. The macro bar shows the day’s protein/carb/fat ratio against a target ratio, which totals alone cannot tell you.

**Why it exists.** A calorie total cannot tell you the day was wrong. The ratio between protein, carbs and fat can.

**How to use it.**

1. Pick foods from the library (American and Indian staples), or set calories and macros directly.
2. Read the macro bar against the target ratio rather than the totals.
3. Log as you eat. A day reassembled from memory at 11pm is fiction with decimal places.

### Challenges

`?view=challenges`

**What it is.** Fixed-length disciplines (75 Hard/Soft, 90-day, custom). Check in against each day’s rules; a progress ring and week calendar track you. Strict mode resets to Day 1 on a miss.

**Why it exists.** A fixed-length discipline with rules is a different animal from a habit, and pretending otherwise is why most attempts at 75 Hard end quietly.

**How to use it.**

1. Pick 75 Hard, 75 Soft, the 90-day block, or write your own rules.
2. Check in against each day’s rules — the ring and the week calendar do the counting.
3. Choose strict mode deliberately: it resets you to Day 1 on a miss, which is either the point or the thing that ends the attempt.

### Recovery

`?view=nofap`

**What it is.** An opt-in abstinence streak: current streak, personal best, milestones, an urge-surfing counter, and a judgement-free relapse log. Stays entirely on your device.

**Why it exists.** A streak counter with nowhere to write the relapse is a counter you stop opening after the first bad day.

**How to use it.**

1. Turn it on in Settings → Profile. It is off unless you ask for it.
2. Read the current streak beside the personal best; milestones mark the ones worth noticing.
3. Use the urge counter when it passes, and the relapse log when it does not. There is no judgement written into either.

### Cycle

`?view=cycle`

**What it is.** An opt-in, private basal-temperature and cycle chart with free-form flags. Honours your °F/°C unit. Nothing is shared or predicted · just a calm record.

**Why it exists.** A basal-temperature record is only useful as an unbroken line — a missed week is a gap that cannot be filled in later.

**How to use it.**

1. Turn it on in Settings → Profile.
2. Enter today’s temperature. It honours the °F/°C unit you set.
3. Add free-form flags for anything worth remembering. Nothing here is predicted, scored or shared.

### Home Workout

`?view=homeworkout`

**What it is.** A no-equipment home workout hub. Browse a curated bodyweight library · each exercise has a target muscle, how-to cues, a professional YouTube demo, and a YouTube search fallback. Build a session, log reps/sets/time, and save it as a Home workout that feeds your Fitness totals and history.

**Why it exists.** “No equipment” is the most common reason a session does not happen. This removes the excuse rather than arguing with it.

**How to use it.**

1. Reach it from Fitness → Bodyweight exercise library.
2. Pick exercises — each has its target muscle, how-to cues and a demo video.
3. Log reps, sets and time. It feeds your Fitness totals and history like any other session.

## Mind

### Mindset

`?view=mindset`

**What it is.** A toolkit for your mental game and thinking style, app-wide (not just sport). Browse principles across focus, resilience, growth, composure, confidence, discipline and connection; pick a few to actively work on and jot how you’ll apply each. Build the habit of thinking well.

**Why it exists.** How you talk to yourself about a bad week decides whether there is a good one after it — and that is trainable.

**How to use it.**

1. Browse the principles across focus, resilience, growth, composure, confidence, discipline and connection.
2. Pick two or three to actively work on. Ten is the same as none.
3. Write how you will apply each in your own words. A principle you cannot phrase yourself is one you have not taken.

### Reading

`?view=reading`

**What it is.** Your reading log as three shelves · want-to-read, currently-reading (with a page-progress bar), and finished (with a 1–5 star rating). Set a yearly book goal; pages read and books finished this year roll up into Insights and Goals.

**Why it exists.** Three shelves is the entire model — what you want, what you are inside, what you finished — and it is enough.

**How to use it.**

1. Add a book to want-to-read the moment you hear about it.
2. Move it to reading and nudge the page count; the progress bar is what gets books finished.
3. Rate it 1–5 on the way out. Set a yearly goal and pages and books roll up into Insights and Goals.

**Why it exists.** Not everything belongs to a date. A collection is the page for everything that does not.

**How to use it.**

1. Open the Future Log to see everything you have dated ahead of today, in one list.

### Focus

`?view=focus`

**What it is.** A developer work tracker. Log coding sessions (time, project, flow, stress, interruptions, languages) and see weekly hours, a streak, a minutes chart, cumulative hours, language bars, and a focus↔stress insight.

**Why it exists.** Deep work never shows up in a habit grid, because the number that matters is not that it happened but how it went.

**How to use it.**

1. Log a coding session: time, project, flow, stress, interruptions, languages.
2. Read the weekly hours and the minutes chart rather than the streak.
3. Check the focus↔stress insight. It is the one on this page that changes what you do next week.

## Insights

### Insights

`?view=insights`

**What it is.** Reflection hub: streaks, task completion, correlation patterns, a month index, and full-text search (filter by type). The Weekly Review walks you through migrate → review → reflect; the Tag manager renames/merges #tags.

**Why it exists.** A journal you never read back is a diary, not a tool. This is the page that reads it back to you — and some things are only visible as a shape, which is why every chart lives here too.

**How to use it.**

1. Pick a domain chip first — Overview, Mood & sleep, Habits, Body, Tasks, Records. It is faster than scrolling, and the count on each chip tells you what is down there.
2. Scan the activity heatmap before anything else. The empty weeks are the finding.
3. Read the correlation matrix. A weak cell is a result: "sleep does nothing for my mood" is exactly what a list of strong correlations can never tell you.
4. Search once for both — the box matches your entries AND the panels on this page, so "sleep debt" finds the chart and "gym" finds the entries.
5. Run the Weekly Review once a week: migrate → review → reflect, in that order.

## Your journal

### Account

`?view=account`

**What it is.** bujo has no accounts — no email, no password, no provider. Here you set a local name and face (stored in this journal, on this device, and not checked against anything), turn on end-to-end encrypted sync with a single passphrase if you want this journal on another device, and find the issue tracker. To lock the journal itself, use the passcode in Settings → Sync & privacy. See docs/AUTH.md.

**Why it exists.** There is no account here. This page exists to say so — and to offer the one thing an account is normally for: getting this journal onto a second device.

**How to use it.**

1. Set a local name and face. It is stored in this journal, on this device, and checked against nothing.
2. Turn on end-to-end encrypted sync with a single passphrase only if you want this journal elsewhere.
3. To lock the journal itself, use the passcode in Settings → Sync & privacy. That is a different thing from sync.

### Settings

`?view=settings`

**What it is.** Profile/units, journal feel (paper, handwriting, accent, Today-dashboard cards), reminders, and Data & Cloud · storage meter, JSON/Markdown/CSV export, print, passcode encryption, and cloud sync.

**Why it exists.** Your journal lives in this browser and nowhere else. Nothing else in this app matters if you have not exported a backup.

**How to use it.**

1. Export JSON today, and again every month. Clearing browser data deletes the journal, and there is no copy to ask anyone for.
2. Set units, theme and the journal feel — paper texture, handwriting, accent, and which cards Today shows.
3. Turn on the Cycle or Recovery tracker here if you want them. Both are off unless you ask.
4. “Load demo data” fills about thirty days of samples so every chart has something in it. It replaces the current journal, and offers you a backup first.

### Your first five minutes

One day logged end to end, so the app has something real in it. · **5 minutes, once**

1. **Load the demo and look around** — Settings → Demo & reset → Load demo data fills about thirty days, so every chart has something in it. Do this before you write anything of your own: it replaces the journal. *(`?view=settings`)*
2. **Write three lines in Today** — One task, one event, one note. Type "t buy milk", then "e dentist 3pm", then "n slept badly". Enter after each. *(`?view=today`)*
3. **Close a task** — Click the · glyph beside a task and it becomes ✕. Click again to cycle through migrated and dropped. The status is the glyph — there is no separate checkbox anywhere in this app. *(`?view=today`)*
4. **Rate the day** — Set mood, stress and sleep on the 0–10 scales. Three taps. These are what every correlation and chart is built from, so a day without them is a blank in Stats. *(`?view=today`)*
5. **Export a backup** — Settings → Data → Export JSON. Your journal exists only in this browser. Do it now, while it costs nothing, so the habit is in place before the journal is worth losing. *(`?view=settings`)*

### Your first week

A daily loop that survives a bad day, and two habits you can actually see. · **about a minute a day**

1. **Pick two habits. Two.** — Trackers → add habit. Two is not a soft start, it is the right number — a grid with eleven rows is a grid you stop filling in on day four. *(`?view=trackers`)*
2. **Anchor the log to something you already do** — Decide when you write: with coffee, or before bed. A journal with no fixed time is one you do at 1am once a week and then stop.
3. **Tick the grid and rate the day, every day** — Tap the habit cells, set mood/stress/sleep. Under a minute. Skipping the written entries is fine; skipping the three numbers is what leaves holes in every chart later. *(`?view=trackers`)*
4. **Log one session of whatever you train** — Fitness for anything timed, Strength for sets and reps. One session is enough to make the week view mean something. *(`?view=fitness`)*
5. **On day seven, run the Weekly Review** — Insights → Weekly Review walks you through migrate → review → reflect. This is the step that turns a pile of entries into something you have actually read. *(`?view=insights`)*

### Your first month

The migration ritual, the charts that finally have enough data, and a backup you trust. · **twenty minutes at month end**

1. **Migrate, and drop things on purpose** — Plan → work the overdue list. Move each task to today or tomorrow, or drop it. Dropping is not failure: a task carried for five weeks was never going to happen, and was costing you attention every time you read past it. *(`?view=plan`)*
2. **Read the heatmap before anything else** — Insights → filter to Habits → activity heatmap. The empty weeks are the finding. Everything else on that page is detail about the weeks that were not empty. *(`?view=insights`)*
3. **Check the sleep↔mood scatter** — Insights → filter to Mood & sleep. Thirty days is roughly where this becomes readable. Most people are confidently wrong about their own answer, which is the reason to look rather than guess. *(`?view=insights`)*
4. **Set the new month up** — Monthly → record where you are, what you want from the month, and the photo. Two minutes, and it is what makes the month findable a year from now. *(`?view=monthly`)*
5. **Export again, and keep the file** — Settings → Export JSON, and Export Markdown if you want it readable in Obsidian or Logseq. A monthly export is the entire backup strategy, and it is yours to run — no server is holding a copy. *(`?view=settings`)*
